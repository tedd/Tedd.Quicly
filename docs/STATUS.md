# Implementation status

Last updated 2026-10-02. Test counts are totals across both target frameworks (net11.0 + net10.0), measured per project and
per framework on the branch `fix/localhost-drops` — `main` at 19cb971 (the admission fix: the Hello waits for a
definitive datagram capability and the admission deadline also binds late Hello, HelloAck and policy completion;
[CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md)) plus the localhost-drop fixes and their reviews ([RELEASE-NOTES.md](RELEASE-NOTES.md)): the sequence clock for idle keys, expiry from the first
scheduler pass, the transport outcome statistics, the bounded drain backlog of undrained unreliable channels and `Release`
after `Dispose`; and, merged last (e21c30f), the MsQuic held-stream resume fix: a stream the receiver held (receive ring
full, budget used up, application not polling) and resumed with zero bytes could stall for good, its messages reported
`Delivered` to the sender — a hold is now answered to MsQuic as a partial consumption and the resume only re-enables
receiving. The group loss the release of 2026-09-30 lists as a known defect — a `ReliableUnordered` receiver that fell
behind reset group streams the sender had already reported `Delivered` — is fixed after it: `MaxGroups` is a
sender's bound, and a receiver accepts every group the connection's stream limit admits (PROTOCOL.md §7). On top of that
this status carries the per-channel receive credit of the reliable stream channels (an undrained reliable channel holds back
only its own streams), a channel that stays in order when a handler drains or registers inside a Poll, `Drain` keeping a
compressed reliable message it cannot decode yet, and the MsQuic transport answering `StreamLimitReached` for a send that
loses the race against the peer's stream limit (RELEASE-NOTES.md, "Unreleased"), and the fixes of the third review round
of that stack (branch `fix/stack-review`, with `main`'s 5ab528b merged in): a compressed message of a reliable channel no
handler reads staged in a block of its decoded size, taken all or nothing at its start, and decoded in place
(`Lz4Block.TryDecompressInPlace`, which keeps the in-place margin on its stack, so the block is the decoded size's own
size class: RC2R1-1), so no decode waits for a buffer and `Drain` waits only for the decode rate (second recheck round,
RC2-1); other compressed messages decoded in place when their block fits, else into a second buffer that
may take the receive budget past its limit by one buffer, tried once; `Drain` keeping a reliable channel's order while a
message waits for its decode; the half of the budget shared by the reliable channels no handler reads, counted peer-wide by
the messages taken while their channel was limited (constant time per message, RC2-2), a pended-stream ring bounded by the ring's slots and the free budget and grown for reset streams, an MsQuic client
that grants no streams before admission, the stream table's growth cap, ReliableLatest's refused-start slot and
`OutOfMemory` retry, and four Bulk receive records per transfer. Line coverage figures are from the module reviews (Microsoft.Testing.Extensions.CodeCoverage,
cobertura; the Core rows were re-measured at the wave C2 merge) and were not re-measured after the hot-path pass or the
2026-09-30 fixes; no benchmark was re-run for those fixes either (see Remaining). The Core, Testing, Transport.MsQuic,
Server and Client rows were measured on `fix/stack-review` after the fixes of the first review round of RC2 (RC2R1-1 to
RC2R1-3), unfiltered, in Release, one project at a time, with the test process pinned to four cores
(`DOTNET_PROCESSOR_COUNT=4`), and all five pass on both frameworks there (Transport.MsQuic: 2 skipped per framework). One
Core test was left out of those runs: `CompletionTableReviewTests.Review_Late_Complete_Racing_Release_And_Reallocation_Must_Not_Touch_The_New_Occupant`
(sixteen spinning threads; see Remaining). On four cores it sometimes does not finish. In this round it hung in 1 of 1
unfiltered Core run and in 3 of 3 runs alone (180 s limit). The reviewer's one unfiltered run on four cores finished
(2 633 of 2 633). Core is 2 637 per framework without it and 2 638 with it; the Core total counts it. In the first gate pass,
`ReviewRecheck2R1WorkSignalTests.A_Host_That_Sleeps_On_The_Signal_Never_Sleeps_Over_Waiting_Work` failed its own
precondition (fewer than 1 000 wake-ups) on both frameworks. A spinning probe was running on other cores at the time.
It passed in the unfiltered reruns and alone, on both frameworks. The EndToEnd and Replication rows are from the third review
round's fixes on the same branch, and the other rows were not re-measured for this update. The receive credit's cost was measured only on the
`Drain` path (paired, net10.0: 2.5 to 7.6 % per message in `SessionDrainBench`), before the recheck round added a pass over the
accounted channels to each message start of a drained channel (the shared half). That pass is constant time since RC2-2: a
scratch probe of `ReceiveCredit` alone (take, count and return of one message of a drained channel, one core, best of nine)
gives 9 to 10 ns per message start with 2, 8, 32 or 128 channels, where the review measured 11 to 13 ns at 2 and 175 to
273 ns at 128 before. The in-place decode is within 0 to 15 % of the out-of-place decode alone, and keeping the margin on
the stack costs no more than the shared machine's noise ([primitives](benchmarks/primitives.md)). `SessionDrainBench`, the handler path and the group streams were not measured
with a paired A/B yet (the machine was shared with concurrent sessions; see Remaining). (The certificate/TLS tests had failed on this machine since test roots with
fixed names piled up in the Windows intermediate store past its 50-per-name limit; roots are now unique and removed after use.)
