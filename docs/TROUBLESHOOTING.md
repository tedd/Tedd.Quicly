# Troubleshooting

Every way the library can drop a message is counted. This page maps a symptom to the counter that names its
cause. The counters are read with `QuiclyPeer.GetStatistics(out PeerStatistics)` and
`QuiclyPeer.GetChannelStatistics(channel, out ChannelStatistics)`; both are allocation-free, so a host can
sample them every second and log the differences. All of them are totals since the peer was created and
survive an in-place reconnect.

## Messages go missing on localhost: which counter tells you why

Loopback loses no packets, so when an unreliable channel's `Sent` on one end is larger than `Received` on the
other, one of the two ends dropped the difference on purpose and counted it. Take a snapshot on **both** ends,
for the channel and for the peer, and look the difference up here. "Sender" and "receiver" are the two ends of
the channel in question.

| Look at | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| sender `ChannelStatistics.TransportCanceled`, `PeerStatistics.DatagramsCanceled` | The sender's transport dropped the datagram before transmission. Unreliable datagrams are handed over with the transport's cancel-on-blocked flag (`PeerOptions.DropWhenBlocked`, default on), which MsQuic documents as "a frame should be dropped when it can't be sent immediately"; a datagram that no longer fits after the path's datagram limit shrank is cancelled the same way. The send completes `DeliveryStatus.Expired`, but the channel's `Expired` counter does not move. Per channel the unit is messages (every member of a dropped packed container, every fragment); per peer it is datagrams. | Look at `PeerStatistics.Transport` (`CongestionEvents`, `SendSuspectedLostPackets`, the congestion window and RTT) for what the transport thought of the path at the time. Send less per tick, or spread a burst over ticks. If late data is better than no data for you, set `PeerOptions.DropWhenBlocked = false`: blocked datagrams then wait in the transport's queue and are sent when it can send again, however old they have become. That has two prices. A sustained overload grows the transport's queue instead of shedding load. And on an `UnreliableSequenced` channel a queued datagram can be overtaken by a later `Immediate` or high-priority one and arrive after it; the receiver drops it as stale (the mode working), unless more than 1 024 later messages of the channel (65 536 on a 32-bit channel) overtook it and the channel then stayed quiet for two seconds, in which case it is delivered out of order once (`SequenceResyncs`, PROTOCOL.md §8) — so on a sequenced channel keep the default, or do not mix `Immediate` and buffered sends. Fragmented messages are the most exposed to the cancel flag: one of N datagrams dropped loses the whole message. |
| sender `ChannelStatistics.TransportLost`, `PeerStatistics.DatagramsLost` | The sender's transport declared the datagram lost. The send completes `DeliveryStatus.Lost` (a ReliableLatest value is retransmitted instead, see `Retries`). This is the transport's verdict, not a fact about the receiver: a datagram declared lost can still have arrived — on loopback, where nothing is really lost, suspect a stall (a debugger break, a long pause, a suspended process) that delayed the acknowledgements. | Compare with the receiver's `Received`: if the receiver got them, nothing was lost. Stays 0 on a carrier that reports no datagram states (WebTransport). |
| sender `ChannelStatistics.Expired` | The message expired before it was handed to the transport: the scheduler held it back — the send cap, datagrams unavailable, a stream that was blocked — for longer than the channel's expiry. The expiry counts from the message's first scheduler pass (PROTOCOL.md §4.5), so a long frame or a late `Flush` alone no longer expires anything. | Raise `ChannelOptions.ExpiryMicros` (0 = never) or `SendOptions.ExpiryMicros`, or look at what held the scheduler back (`PeerOptions.MaxSendBytesPerSecond`). |
| receiver `ChannelStatistics.Dropped`, `PeerStatistics.SequenceResyncs` | `UnreliableSequenced` and `ReliableLatest` deliver only values newer than the last one accepted for the key, so a value that arrives out of order, or twice, is dropped as stale and counted in `Dropped`. That is the mode working, not a loss. A key that idled for a long time while other keys of the channel stayed busy is **not** dropped any more: staleness is decided on the channel's sequence clock (PROTOCOL.md §8). `SequenceResyncs` counts the times an `UnreliableSequenced` receiver took a sequence that read as old for a jump forward of half the sequence space or more (a long blackout, or a sender whose messages expired unsent): the sequence was more than the reorder window behind the channel's newest (1 024 on a 16-bit channel, 65 536 on a 32-bit one) and arrived more than two seconds after the newest last advanced. It is normally 0. A value that arrives late but inside the window is simply stale and counted in `Dropped`, however late it is. | A steadily rising `Dropped` with no reordering on the path means the two ends disagree about the channel table or an old build is on one end; `Dropped` that follows `Immediate` sends on a congested link is the sender's own queue being overtaken (first row). A rising `SequenceResyncs` without blackouts means messages arrive more than two seconds late *and* more than a window of later ones overtook them: the sender runs with `DropWhenBlocked` off, or packs sequenced messages next to `ReliableLatest` values, on a link that stays congested for seconds. |
| receiver `ChannelStatistics.DrainQueueDrops`, `PeerStatistics.DrainQueueDrops` | An **unreliable** channel (`UnreliableUnordered` / `UnreliableSequenced` without `CoalesceOnReceive`) has no handler, and what was queued for it was **still there when the next `Poll` began**: nobody drained the channel empty in between. From that moment its queue is *backlog*, and backlog is bounded: the backlogs of all such channels together may occupy the part of the drain-queue pool that is not reserved for reliable channels (the pool is `ReceiveRingCapacity` messages but never more than 1 024, so at the default ring of 4 096 it is 1 024; half of it is reserved when the table has a `ReliableOrdered` or `ReliableUnordered` channel) and may pin a quarter of `ReceiveBudgetBytes`, charged in buffer block sizes. With every option at its default that is 512 messages (1 024 without a reliable channel in the table) and 64 KiB — 1 024 messages of up to 64 bytes, 256 of 65 to 256 bytes, 42 of 257 to 1 536 bytes. The `Poll` cuts the backlog to that, and afterwards the **oldest** queued message is dropped for each new one: that of the backlogged channel furthest over its even share (the channel's own when no other holds more). The counter is on the channel that lost the message; `Received` still counts it. A channel that is drained every frame, and a channel with a handler, never lose anything here — not to their own bursts, not to another channel's backlog, however many channels are drained that way, and not what the server sent with its admission (the `Poll` inside `QuiclyClient.ConnectAsync` counts as drained). Once a channel is backlog it stays backlog until a `Drain` finds or leaves its queue empty. | Register a handler for the channel, or `Drain` it **completely, once per `Poll`**: call `Drain` until it returns 0 (or pass a span it does not fill), every frame, before or after the `Poll`. A channel drained that way keeps a burst of any size the receive ring and `ReceiveBudgetBytes` took. The counter rises for a host that polls several times between two drains (only what the *last* Poll queued is safe) — typically a server that calls `PollAll` at network rate and drains once per simulation tick — that drains with a fixed span and does not call again, or that does not read the channel at all. If you cannot drain once per Poll, raise `ReceiveBudgetBytes` (the byte bound of the backlog is a quarter of it); `ReceiveRingCapacity` does **not** raise the node bound beyond 1 024. A channel you do not read at all should not be sent on: it also keeps the receive ring closed for one Poll interval the first time a burst fills the pool (each such channel once; not again until it has been drained; two intervals for a channel that was drained before, or when the burst arrives with the session's first Poll). |
| receiver `ChannelStatistics.RingDrops` / `OutOfBuffers`, `PeerStatistics.ReceiveRingDrops` / `OutOfReceiveBuffers`, `ReceiveRingHighWater`, a rising `StreamReceivePends` | The receiver did not take messages out fast enough: the receive ring was full, or the receive budget (`PeerOptions.ReceiveBudgetBytes`) was used up, and complete messages were dropped on arrival. This affects every channel of the peer at once. Two causes. (1) `Poll` is not called often enough for the traffic. (2) A **reliable** channel (`ReliableOrdered`, `ReliableUnordered`) receives traffic but has no handler and is never drained: a reliable message is never dropped, so once the drain-queue pool is full the peer holds the next one and stops emptying the ring. From then on datagrams of every ring channel are dropped here (`RingDrops`), every stream channel is back-pressured (`StreamReceivePends` keeps rising; the sender's reliable sends back up behind QUIC flow control), responses on request/response channels wait so requests time out, and `HasPendingWork` stays `true`. Nothing of a `ReliableOrdered` channel is lost this way (a `ReliableUnordered` channel can lose groups while its receiver is this far behind, see "Reliable channels" below), and coalescing and `ReliableLatest` handlers keep running. The held streams are taken up again when the receiver catches up: the stall in which a stream held over MsQuic was never resumed is fixed (RELEASE-NOTES.md). An undrained *unreliable* channel does not cause this beyond one Poll interval per such channel (two for a channel that was drained before, or when the burst arrives with the session's first Poll; row above). | For (2): register a handler for every reliable channel the other end sends on, or drain it — the stall ends as soon as the channel is drained or gets a handler. To find the channel: it is a reliable channel without a handler whose `Received` stopped rising while the sender keeps sending on it (the sender's `QueuedMessages` / `InFlightMessages` for that channel stay up). For (1): call `Poll` every tick; raise `ReceiveRingCapacity` / `ReceiveBudgetBytes` if `ReceiveRingHighWater` comes close to `ReceiveRingCapacity` under normal load. |
| `ChannelStatistics.SendKeyTableFull` (sender), `ReceiveKeyTableFull` (receiver) | The channel's key table is full. A `ReliableLatest` channel never evicts a live key: the sender refuses the send with `SendStatus.KeyTableFull`, and a receiver whose table is full drops the value and tells the sender (`LatestReject`), which retries within its budget and completes the value `Failed` when the budget is used up. An `UnreliableSequenced` receiver evicts its least recently updated key instead and counts nothing here, except for a key outside a dense key space. | Raise `ChannelOptions.MaxKeys`, or retire keys that are gone (`RetireKey`). |
| receiver `PeerStatistics.DecodeFailures` | A compressed message was dropped in `Poll` or `Drain`: it did not decode, or decoding it would have exceeded the decode budget (`PeerOptions.DecodedBytesPerSecond`, default 8 MiB/s) or the receive budget (the decoded payload needs a second buffer of its raw size). One case is not a drop: `Drain` on a `ReliableOrdered` / `ReliableUnordered` channel leaves a message queued when there is no buffer to decode it into and returns fewer messages than the span holds — release what you got and drain again. | Raise `DecodedBytesPerSecond` if the traffic is legitimate; otherwise compress less (`ChannelOptions.MinCompressSize`). |
| receiver `PeerStatistics.ReassembliesExpired`, `ReassembliesAbandoned`, `FragmentsDropped` | Part of a fragmented message did not arrive — its datagram was cancelled or lost on the sender (first two rows) — and the receiver gave the partial up after 2 × RTT + 100 ms, or a newer value of the key or the channel's reassembly cap replaced it. | See the first two rows; keep fragmented messages rare, or move large payloads to a reliable channel. |
| receiver `ChannelStatistics.ReceiveTooLarge`, `PeerStatistics.MalformedDatagrams`, `DroppedBeforeAdmission` | The message exceeded a size limit, the datagram did not parse against this end's channel table, or it arrived before the session was admitted. | Check that both ends run the same channel table (`ChannelTable.Hash`) and size limits. |
| none of the above, and `Sent` is still larger than `Received` | The datagrams are in flight, or the connection closed or was reconnected with them outstanding: what the transport cancels because the connection is closing completes `Disconnected` and is deliberately not counted as a transport drop. | Compare snapshots taken while the connection is up and idle. |

### How the sender's counters add up

On a transport that reports datagram send states (MsQuic; not the WebTransport carrier):

* per peer, in datagrams: `DatagramsSent = DatagramsAcknowledged + DatagramsLost + DatagramsCanceled`
  + in flight now + ended by a close or a reconnect;
* per channel, in messages: `Sent − TransportCanceled` is what the transport put on the wire or still holds, and
  `Sent − TransportCanceled − TransportLost` is what was acknowledged or is in flight (or was ended by a close or
  a reconnect).

`Sent` and `BytesSent` never go back: a message the transport later dropped was still handed to it. The outcome
counters move when `Poll` or `Flush` drains the transport's report, so they lag the event by up to one tick. A
blocked drop whose report was still waiting when `Close` started is not counted.

### Reliable channels

A `ReliableOrdered` or `Bulk` message is never dropped while the connection lives; it can
only fail to be admitted (`SendStatus`, and the channel's `QueueFull` / `TooLarge` counters) or end
`Disconnected` with the connection. The price of "never dropped" is on the receiver: a reliable channel that
has no handler and is not drained eventually holds up **all** receiving on that peer — see the
`ReceiveRingDrops` row above. Every reliable channel in the table needs a handler or a `Drain` call. A `ReliableLatest` value is delivered unless a newer value of its key
replaced it (`SendSuperseded`, `ReceiveSuperseded`) — and a tracked value completes `Delivered` only when the
receiver acknowledged exactly the version that was last transmitted (PROTOCOL.md §4.4).

**`ReliableUnordered`: whole groups missing, receiver `PeerStatistics.StreamsReset` rising.** This is a known
defect that is not fixed in this release. A `ReliableUnordered` channel behaves like the other two, with one
exception: when the receiver falls far behind — it does not poll for a while, its receive ring or
`ReceiveBudgetBytes` is full, or the channel is not drained — groups can be lost. The sender frees a group's
stream once QUIC acknowledged it, the receiver still counts that stream open until it has processed it, so the
sender opens more group streams than the receiver accepts (`ChannelOptions.MaxGroups`, 8 by default; over MsQuic
a client's handshake grant of 1 024 streams is also larger than the receiver's records). The receiver resets the
streams beyond its limit and counts each in `StreamsReset`; the sender has already completed their messages
`Delivered` and none of its counters moves. `StreamsReset` also counts streams reset for other reasons (a
malformed or stalled stream, a Bulk transfer beyond its limit), so compare it with the channel's `Sent` on the
sender and `Received` on the receiver. What to do until the fix ships (it is in review, for the next release):
poll every tick, give the channel a handler, and put messages that must survive a receiver hitch on a
`ReliableOrdered` channel. A larger `MaxGroups` moves the point at which groups are lost; it does not remove it.

**A reliable stream that stopped for good while the connection stayed up** (the sender reported `Delivered`,
nothing arrived, no counter moved) was the MsQuic held-stream stall of earlier releases: a stream the receiver
held because its ring or budget was full was sometimes never resumed. It is fixed on the receiving end; if you
still see it, check that the receiver runs a package published on or after 2026-09-30.

### Messages arrive late on a host that sleeps between wake-ups

Nothing is dropped and no counter moves, but a handler runs only at the host's next timer. The work signal
(`PeerOptions.WorkSignal`, `IPeerWorkSignal.OnWork`) is an edge: the peer calls it for the first work it
publishes and then stays silent until the edge is re-armed. Two calls re-arm it: `Poll`, and a `HasPendingWork`
that answers `false`. A host loop that does one of the two after every wake-up — poll, or ask the probe and
sleep again when it says there is nothing — cannot miss a publication. A loop that wakes and then does neither
(it only calls `Drain`, say, because the signal was for a channel it reads that way) gets no second signal until
its next `Poll`. `HasPendingWork` does not report messages that wait for `Drain` (in a drain queue or in the
mailbox of a coalescing or `ReliableLatest` channel without a handler): drain those channels on the host's own
tick or on the signal.
